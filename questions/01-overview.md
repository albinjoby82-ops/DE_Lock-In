### M1: True or false

1. COLOSSUS was the first transistor computer.
2. ENIAC was programmed with switches and jumper cables.
3. The transistor was invented at Bell Labs in 1948.
4. LSI means 100 to 1,000 gates per chip.
5. The Intel 4004 had about 2,300 transistors.
6. The popular form of Moore's law says transistor count doubles every 18 to 24 months.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. **False.** COLOSSUS used valves. TX-0 was the first transistor computer. (§1, §2)
2. **True.** (§1)
3. **True.** Bardeen, Brattain and Shockley. (§2)
4. **False.** That's MSI. LSI is 1,000 to 10,000. (§3)
5. **True.** On a 2 × 4 mm die, in 1971. (§4)
6. **True.** (§5)

</details>

### M3: Short answer

State Moore's original 1965 prediction, and the popular form used today.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

**1965:** components per chip double roughly every year. About 65,000 per chip by 1975.

**Today:** transistor count doubles every 18 to 24 months.

From: §5

</details>

### M4: Explain

Explain why Moore's law became a self-fulfilling prophecy.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

It began as an observation and forecast.

As it became widely accepted, the whole industry used it as a goal.

Each company assumed that if it didn't keep pace, a rival would.

So companies worked hard to meet it, which made it come true.

From: §5

</details>

### M5: Short answer

What did John von Neumann change, and what two benefits did it bring?

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

He moved program storage from external to internal.

1. Speed was limited by the electronic logic, not by the reader.
2. The computer could make decisions based on results and change what it did.

From: §1

</details>

---

### M6: Short answer

Give the two main design goals for a digital IC.

<details><summary>Answer</summary>

1. Minimise the number of transistors (size and cost).
2. Minimise the propagation delay (speed).

From: §6

</details>
